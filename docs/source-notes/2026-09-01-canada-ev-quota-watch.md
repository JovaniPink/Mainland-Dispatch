## Question and decision

This review asks whether newly available Canadian quota records change the
August 30 conclusion about China-origin electric vehicles, Canadian admission,
USMCA origin, entry into the United States, or transshipment proof.

They change the factual quota record. They do not change the transshipment
conclusion. Global Affairs Canada now reports actual first-period quota use and
has issued the operative notice for the second period. Neither record identifies
a vehicle entering the United States, establishes USMCA qualification, or
records a customs violation.

## Definitions preserved

- Canadian admission means a China-origin EV entered Canada under a valid
  shipment-specific permit and the applicable Canadian tariff treatment.
- Canadian production requires evidence about manufacturing or assembly in
  Canada. Admission alone is not production.
- USMCA qualification requires the applicable product rule, certification, and
  content calculation. It does not follow from Canadian admission or ownership.
- US entry requires an entry record. It does not follow from Canadian quota use.
- A customs violation requires a responsible determination or comparable legal
  record. Policy concern and modeled exposure are not findings of fraud.

## Disposition

**Effect on the August 30 conclusion:** changes scope, with no verified change
to the conclusion.

The quota is no longer only a ceiling or announced channel: responsible-
government data report 15,603 first-period uses. That observed Canadian import
activity strengthens the record for studying the admission gate. It does not
complete the production, USMCA-origin, or US-entry gates and does not commission
the proposed Inquiry 09.

## Required follow-up

1. Recheck the utilization report after cancellations, late accounting, or a
   first-period revision.
2. Record the second-period utilization series when Global Affairs Canada
   publishes it.
3. Recheck the September 8 countertariff record on or after its effective time
   for an operative notice, amendment, suspension, or replacement.
4. Continue to require VIN-linked production, origin, certification, and entry
   evidence before making a vehicle-specific USMCA or US-entry claim.
5. Preserve this note as a dated snapshot; later evidence receives a new entry
   rather than overwriting these figures.
