## Editorial question

What kind of technological openness is China offering, who benefits from it,
what dependencies accompany it, and where does that openness stop when it
conflicts with Party control or strategic advantage?

Xi Jinping’s AI-governance proposal is the inquiry’s spine. Typhoon Noul tests
mobilization and information quality; Hong Wang and Yu Deng test how scientific
achievement moves through Chinese, European, and American institutions. No
hidden motive is presented as verified original reporting.

## Findings retained

- Xi officially announced 5,000 training opportunities, cooperation centers
  with six international groupings, and MAZU access for 30 countries.
- China’s Foreign Ministry reported that representatives of 29 countries
  signed the WAICO founding agreement; Reuters separately reported the
  signing. Establishment is supported, while institutional substance remains
  unproven.
- China’s public generative-AI services operate under binding political
  content requirements.
- Associated Press reported more than 801,000 Guangdong residents relocated
  during Noul. The inquiry uses this only as a mobilization measure.
- Contemporaneous reporting described Wang and Deng as the first Chinese
  nationals to receive Fields Medals; the IMU controls the award record. Their
  careers are described as transnational.
- Stanford’s AI Index reported a 2.7% leading-model gap as of March 2026.

## Findings qualified

- China is seeking AI leadership and institutional influence; “global
  dominance” is stronger than the reviewed evidence.
- Official Noul information existed. Completeness and independent
  accountability remain open.
- “First Chinese Fields Medalists” is replaced with “first PRC nationals.”
- Possible model-access restrictions are reported deliberations, not policy.
