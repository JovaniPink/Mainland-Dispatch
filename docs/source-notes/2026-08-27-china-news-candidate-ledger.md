This ledger does not compare or rank policy issues, recommend coverage, or decide what the site should publish. It summarizes what the cited third-party and primary web sources do and do not establish. Official Chinese sources below document government data, rules, and positions. They do not independently validate their own promotional or causal claims.

## Evidence contract

A bounded research packet is complete only when the ledger has:

1. A primary source for the central number, rule, event, or statement.
2. An independent source that adds verification, methodology, or technically useful context.
3. At least one credible alternative reading of what the evidence means.
4. Stable definitions, dates, units, populations, and time windows.
5. A bounded claim that does not silently move from an observed event to a national trend.
6. An explicit limitations section and a publication-day recheck plan.

"Contrasting view" does not mean manufacturing a two-sided dispute. It can be a different denominator, a competing causal hypothesis, a forecast versus an observation, or a challenge to the inference drawn from the same facts.

### Source and claim types

The `Role` field is a source-type tag. Every value begins with one of these controlled types: `Primary`, `Official`, `Independent`, `Technical`, `Model`, `Historical`, or `Discovery`. A qualifier can identify the specific role, such as `record`, `summary`, `reporting`, `dataset`, `analysis`, `methodology`, `literature`, `forecast`, `context`, or `lead`.

The evidence-boundary summary uses these claim types:

- `Observed`: a dated measurement, event, filing, registry state, or rule.
- `Attributed claim`: a statement by a named institution or person that has not been independently established.
- `Modeled`: a result produced from stated assumptions rather than direct observation.
- `Forecast`: a projection about a future period.
- `Inference`: an interpretation that combines evidence but is not directly reported by one authoritative record.
- `Unresolved`: a material fact that the current evidence does not settle.

## Candidate 1: Electricity mix and coal buildout

### Bounded frame

China's coal-fired share of electricity generation fell below half in the first half of 2026, while absolute coal generation and coal-plant capacity still grew. The useful story is the coexistence of rapid renewable expansion, demand growth, coal-system constraints, and continued coal construction. It is not a simple "coal is over" or "the transition failed" story.

### Commentary and contrasting readings

- Reading A: A 49.7 percent coal-fired generation share is a real structural milestone because renewables are serving a larger portion of a growing system.
- Reading B: The share can fall while coal generation rises if total demand rises faster. Capacity additions, generation, utilization, and emissions therefore cannot be used interchangeably.
- Reading C: Continued coal construction may reflect reliability, local investment incentives, long-term coal contracts, or grid and storage constraints. The available sources do not establish a single cause.
- Reading D: Curtailment estimates suggest that grid flexibility and market design may now be as important as raw renewable capacity. The 360 TWh estimate should be attributed to CREA/GEM, not stated as an official observed total.

### Editorial limits and rechecks

- Preserve the first-half time window; do not extrapolate 49.7 percent to full-year 2026.
- Show kW or GW for capacity and kWh or TWh for generation.
- Do not infer emissions from generation share alone.
- Recheck the NEA year-end release before publication after the annual data are available.

### Evidence boundary

The sources support a bounded energy-system analysis. They do not support a simple verdict about the end or failure of coal.

### Commentary and contrasting readings

- Reading A: The survey documents population aging, smaller households, and large-scale mobility using a large, disclosed sample.
- Reading B: A national population decline does not mechanically determine near-term output, productivity, consumption, or fiscal outcomes. Those depend on labor participation, retirement, productivity, migration, and policy.
- Reading C: The November 1 total of 1.40545 billion and the year-end total of 1.40489 billion use different reference dates and estimation procedures. The difference is not evidence of an error without the full reconciliation.
- Reading D: IMF estimates are scenario results. They can illustrate exposure and policy tradeoffs but should not be written as a forecast that must occur.

### Editorial limits and rechecks

- Do not combine mainland-only NBS totals with broader geographic definitions.
- Preserve the survey's sample size and net-undercount disclosure.
- Obtain provincial tables or microdata documentation before building a subnational map.
- Avoid demographic determinism and unsupported causal claims about national power or social behavior.

### Evidence boundary

The sources support a national data presentation. They do not yet support a provincial visualization without a direct table and methodology audit.

## Cross-candidate commentary register

These are the recurring interpretive tensions the site can make explicit:

| Tension                                             | Evidence-safe treatment                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Share versus absolute level                         | Report both; a falling share can coexist with a rising absolute amount    |
| Capacity versus generation                          | Keep power capacity, output, utilization, and emissions separate          |
| Standard issued versus standard adopted             | Seek implementation, conformance, procurement, or usage evidence          |
| Trial registered versus outcome demonstrated        | Registration is pipeline evidence, not efficacy or approval               |
| Demonstration versus deployment                     | Seek duration, intervention, cost, safety, and real-world task data       |
| Forecast versus observation                         | Label model, scenario, projection, and observed data separately           |
| Government position versus independent verification | Attribute official claims and add external evidence where available       |
| Restriction versus ban                              | Quote exact scope, item, end use, destination, and licensing path         |
| Live disaster count versus final toll               | Timestamp, name the authority, and never average conflicting counts       |
| National total versus subnational distribution      | Do not infer local patterns without provincial or local data              |
| Record versus repeatability                         | One result does not establish reliability, reuse, or commercial economics |

## Source-role and commentary rules

- Primary sources establish the actual release, rule, filing, standard, registry record, event result, or official position.
- Independent reporting can verify chronology, surface corrections, and add interviews or observations.
- International institutions provide comparable data, forecasts, and models, but their forecasts remain forecasts.
- Technical literature provides methodology and limitation context, not automatic validation of a current event.
- Company and government claims stay attributed unless an independent dataset tests them.
- Discussion forums and social posts may suggest questions. They are never factual evidence, are not cited publicly, and should not supply usernames or long quotations.
- Search result snippets, aggregators, duplicate syndications, and reposts are discovery aids, not additional corroborating sources.

## Link verification log

The August 27, 2026 recheck used two distinct tests:

1. Every original source URL was opened or inspected through the web research reader to confirm the publisher, title, and evidence carried by the page.
2. Every URL remaining after replacements received a strict HTTPS GET with redirect following, certificate validation, a 25-second total timeout, and a descriptive user agent.

The final 72-link set returned 65 HTTP 200 responses, one HTTP 202 response, and six HTTP 403 responses in the strict GET pass. The 202 response was the admitted Rigzone syndication. The six 403 responses were the SAMR registry page, two IMF pages, and three OECD pages; each remained identifiable and readable through the web research path. No final link produced a certificate error, connection timeout, or HTTP 404 in that pass.

An HTTP 200 response establishes transport availability only. It does not validate the source's claims. A 403 response may reflect automated-client restrictions, so the ledger records it as an access limitation rather than silently treating it as a dead source. Publication-day checks must still reopen all links and reconfirm every live record.

"Log everything" here means every source admitted to the evidence set, every additional candidate developed during the search, every material exclusion, and every unresolved verification step. It does not mean listing every search-result duplicate, social repost, or irrelevant page; those are neither sources nor editorial evidence.
