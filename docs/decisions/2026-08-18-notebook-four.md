# Notebook Four editorial commission

**Status:** Accepted
**Effective:** August 18, 2026
**Entry:** “Routing Around Risk”

## Decision

The editor commissioned a fourth Season Zero Notebook examining China's
response to the 2026 Hormuz and Red Sea crisis. The initiating source set
includes reporting and analysis from Reuters, Al Jazeera, The Guardian, and The
Washington Post, followed through to identified energy, trade, sanctions,
shipping, scientific, and regulatory records.

The Notebook is published as source-backed interpretation. It is not original
reporting, a live shipping monitor, navigation advice, or a verified Dispatch
record. The central conclusion is a portfolio argument: inventories,
pipelines, ship-to-ship transfers, selective passage, and an announced seasonal
Arctic container service perform different functions and cannot be collapsed
into one bypass capacity.

## Editorial model

The inquiry must preserve the source unit, period, and denominator for each
scale claim. Daily petroleum flows, stored barrels, annual tonnes, container
trade shares, and announced sailings may be placed side by side only when the
interface states that they are non-additive.

The Northern Sea Route is evaluated as a limited container hedge and a
China-Russia governance relationship, not as a replacement for Hormuz energy
flows. Total NSR cargo must remain separate from international transit cargo.
An announced schedule must remain separate from voyage-level departure and
arrival evidence.

## Claim decisions

The broad portfolio thesis and the pre-crisis Hormuz scale are retained. China
inventory estimates, pipeline capacity, the announced Arctic schedule, and
Reuters' tanker-transfer reporting are qualified by their measurement and
source limitations.

The submitted 23-container-voyage series, a blended total-versus-transit NSR
cargo measure, and the conflation of Bellona's two different 100-vessel counts
are excluded. Russian intelligence enablement remains separate from direct
command, and PRC-linked commercial procurement remains separate from an
unproved claim of top-level Chinese direction.

## Product scope

The commission authorizes:

- one `maritime-risk` Notebook schema variant with route, point, scale,
  timeline, and source-reference checks;
- one static public Notebook route and its homepage, sitemap, metadata, and
  JSON-LD integrations;
- one click-to-load MapLibre and OpenFreeMap surface whose state transitions
  are owned by XState;
- schematic source-backed corridor geometry with explicit non-AIS and
  non-navigation labels;
- six non-additive scale cards, twelve claim dispositions, four analytical
  turns, ten timeline entries, twenty-four source stops, and three identified
  podcast or video records; and
- desktop and mobile review in the paper and night themes.

It does not authorize live AIS ingestion, proprietary tanker-data
reconstruction, automated source intake, a new top-level product area, a route
forecast, or promotion of withheld source leads into the public Archive.

## Review boundary

Research stopped August 18, 2026 at the public records and exact limitations
recorded in the [Notebook Four source
ledger](../source-notes/2026-08-18-strategic-rebalancing-research-ledger.md).
Future revisions must preserve report-versus-observation distinctions, date new
evidence, update the claim registry and map data through the schema, and rerun
the publication gates. They must not silently convert an announced voyage,
single-source estimate, intelligence report, or schematic corridor into a
verified observation.
